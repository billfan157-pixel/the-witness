THE WITNESS Environment Kit Development Roadmap
Updated 9 Nov 2026 · Revision 3 · AI-led production · Q01 required, L04 extension · Quality gates replace the fixed eight-week deadline. Three.js r187. Phases 1–2 now have regional references, compressed PBR assets, lifecycle/offline evidence and a laptop GPU comparison. The original document is retained as a backup.
1. Goal, scope and assumptions
Goal. Build an authored, reusable Environment Kit and architecture that can actually be explored. Q01 must support a continuous playable route through a doorway, an interior, stairs and an upper floor or roof, and back to the harbor. Visual quality is judged at player eye height and during movement as well as against the cover-art reference.
Required slice and extension:
Q01 "Bến không chờ người về" (Caesarea harbor) — the first scene every player sees.
L04 Galilee shore (Ga 21) is an extension after Q01 meets its gates. It adds basalt, water, boats and nets. It is not a second mandatory scene for an eight-week deadline.
Jerusalem, L01 Exodus, L02 En Gedi, L03 cistern and L05 prison remain backlog. Reuse is an architectural goal, not a claim that those scenes are already kit-ready or dressed.
Confirmed production decisions:
AI performs all build work: scripts, asset generation, conversion, integration, tests and repairs. No human modelling or programming labor is assumed. The old 15–20 hours/week and 120–160 human-hour estimate is withdrawn.
Quality takes priority over a fixed completion date. Each gate requires evidence. AI generation speed does not by itself certify visual quality, historical accuracy or device performance.
The game remains on Three.js. Editable sources and glTF/PBR support reuse; a future engine port would still require gameplay and integration work.
Existing gameplay must be preserved. Run model/source, browser, craft and graphics checks for relevant runtime changes. The graphics and model baseline passed; the full browser campaign suite was not completed in the preceding graphics change.
Outside the current scope: new character art, mocap, WebGPU migration, Temple Mount, full Legends dressing and cinematic VFX rework. The existing L01 fire and sea shaders are retained.

3. Look Bible and evidence rules
Use the existing project evidence vocabulary: [K] Scripture, [L] historical/archaeological source, [G] Catholic teaching, [H] artistic invention, [C] uncertain claim. The original roadmap labels [V] and [D] map to [L] and [H]. Record sources, dates, confidence and reconstruction boundaries per claim.
Target look: assets/journey-cover.webp guides warm light, weathered surfaces, muted earth colours and atmospheric depth. The regional board now records eight source observations per region with original diagrams and external source-image links; six reproducible pre-kit game views per scene provide the comparison baseline. Archaeological photographs are not redistributed without verified rights.
Material families per region are art-direction choices. Mark historically supported claims [L] and distinguish reconstruction [H/C].

Palette rule. No neon or modern saturation; earth tones only, as in your character research (madder red, woad blue, ochre, undyed linen and wool). Sacred Tier presence is shown through light, not extra props.
Region locks control art direction and prevent accidental reuse. They are not absolute archaeological claims that a material could never occur outside its preferred region. Use terrain, season and habitat when selecting vegetation.
4. Technical standards in docs/environment-kit/standards.md
Item
Standard
Units and axes
1 Blender unit = 1 m; Y up on export; pivot at bottom centre of each module
Module grid
Architecture on a 0.5 m grid; wall modules 2 m and 4 m wide, 3 m and 4.5 m tall
Texel density
Terrain 256 px/m; architecture and props 512 px/m; hero props up to 1024 px/m
Texture sets
Albedo (sRGB), normal, ORM packed (occlusion / roughness / metal). Source 2K, ship 1K–2K as KTX2 (UASTC for normals, ETC1S for albedo/ORM)
Triangle budgets
Wall module ≤ 800; column ≤ 1,500; small prop ≤ 3,000; hero prop ≤ 12,000; tree LOD0 ≤ 8,000; rock ≤ 2,500
LODs
LOD where repetition, screen size and measured scene cost justify it; prioritize large repeated assets and vegetation.
Materials
One material per module (trim-sheet or atlas) so draw calls stay low
Vertex colour
AO/wear/wind masks only with an explicit shader contract; ordinary RGB vertex colour remains separate.
Collision
Local extras.footprint circles transformed per node/instance for 2D obstacles; separate 3D collision and support surfaces for accessible buildings.
Naming
ENV_<region>_<type>_<variant>_LOD0, e.g. ENV_CAE_Wall_Ashlar_4m_A_LOD0
Licence
Every external asset gets a row in assets/ART-CREDITS.md: source URL, licence, date, modifications
Per-scene budgets remain provisional targets: desktop 1.2 M visible triangles, 250 draw calls and 256 MiB texture memory; mobile balanced 400 k, 120 and 128 MiB. A dedicated 200-module scene is measured on this laptop’s Intel Arc; headless GPU time and texture upload estimates do not certify full-scene FPS or driver residency.

6. Milestone plan and acceptance gates
Phase 1 Foundations and baseline
Create the Look Bible, standards, catalogue and repeatable Q01/L04 views. Define required Q01 access and distinguish measured device results from emulated checks.
Phase 2 AI asset pipeline
Prove source-to-GLB generation, shared-core loading, instancing, transformed footprints, resource ownership and offline packaging. KTX2, Meshopt and locally bundled codecs now have actual browser tests; retain these checks as the pipeline expands.
Phase 3 Playable architecture blockout
Build harbor to doorway to room to stairs to upper floor or roof and back. Implement 3D support/collision and floor-aware navigation and interaction. Save and resume on the correct floor. Verify first-person and third-person cameras.
Phase 4 Q01 look development
Finish one playable corner with ground, wall base, doorway, quay and props. Review scale, texture repetition, module seams, wear and light at eye height. Do not accept only a beauty screenshot.
Phase 5 Complete Q01
Create only the modules and nature/prop assets needed for the required route and scene framing. Preserve quest reachability and validate each replacement before removing its fallback.
Phase 6 Optimisation and lighter offline build
Tune LOD, textures, foliage and effects. Keep the same essential walkable interiors, stairs and roof access in the lighter build. Test fresh load, save/resume, scene transitions and missing assets with network blocked.
Phase 7 L04 extension
After Q01 passes, extend the kit with basalt, shore, boat and net assets. Define boat boarding or moving-platform behavior separately if included. Preserve Q01 quality.
Phase 8 Final evidence and delivery
Run functional and visual checks, static budgets and device-specific profiling. Store evidence against source/build revisions. Record remaining limits. Other scenes remain backlog.

8. Integration architecture
Loader bundle. WitnessKit.create(T, renderer) creates an owned runtime; init remains available for legacy callers. Addons share engine Three r187. The PBR study has PNG reference, KTX2 plus Meshopt, and a lighter PNG tier. Exact JS/WASM decoders ship locally. Source scripts and encoder checksums make conversion repeatable.
Manifest-driven scenes. Load with scene-generation/cancellation guards, stable placeholders and explicit ownership of shared resources. Keep existing builders until replacement visuals, collision and gameplay are verified. Do not append an asset to a scene that has already been disposed.
Height and support. Use a shared terrain sampler derived from the final terrain geometry, including seabed changes. Multi-level buildings additionally need 3D support surfaces and collision: a single height(x,z) cannot represent both a room floor and its roof.
Collision. Node-local footprints use node and instance transforms. Compressed assets declare footprintSpace=asset so quantized geometry transforms cannot distort authored collision. Footprints remain 2D obstacles. Add 3D walls, floors, ceilings, stairs, camera collision and floor-aware interactions in Phase 3.
Materials. Reuse current PBR terrain. Build only required material families first. Keep shader overlays for documented dust/wear effects. Do not reinterpret the existing AO/roughness/height atlas as a standard metallic ORM map.
Packs and offline. Use versioned region packs and runtime caching with actual cache writes and failure handling. The lighter standalone build may reduce textures, detail and effects, but retains the same essential accessible architecture and progress semantics. Test without network, including decoder/transcoder dependencies.
Shadows and atmosphere. Follow-player 44 x 44 m shadows, GTAO, scene LUTs, height fog and up-to-4x render-target MSAA are already integrated. Tune these against new assets; do not schedule a second implementation without evidence.
9. QA gates and metrics
Functional acceptance: relevant npm test, npm run test:browser, npm run test:craft-details and npm run test:graphics checks. Add route tests for doors, stairs, floors, ceilings, roofs, camera clipping, save/resume and interactions across levels. Missing evidence keeps a gate open.
Visual baseline: six recorded views each of Q01 and L04 before kit dressing, plus first-person and traversal checks. Store camera/player parameters and fixture identity with images so comparisons can be repeated.
Performance: specify actual device, resolution, quality and test duration. Desktop 60 fps and mobile balanced 30 fps remain targets. Record frame-time distribution and repeated scene changes; headless SwiftShader is functional QA, not a phone performance measurement.
Budget checks: test/kit-budget.cjs checks pack/catalog consistency and static GLB budgets during build. Runtime draw calls, residency, render targets and FPS require separate instrumentation; static asset checks cannot certify them.
Licence audit: every external asset has a ledger row before it is committed.

11. Scope decisions and gate evidence
Gate 0: standards, source ledger and repeatable visual baseline. Gate 1: asset pipeline including loading, transformed collision metadata, texture compression and offline dependencies.
Gate 2: a blockout route through an interior, stairs and a roof works in both camera modes, with correct collision, save/resume and floor-aware interactions.
Gate 3: one playable Q01 look-development corner meets the visual standard. Expand Q01 only after this corner is accepted.
Gate 4: complete and optimize Q01, validate the lighter offline build, then decide whether to extend L04. A gate stays open when any required test or quality evidence is missing.
If scope must be reduced, reduce catalog breadth and defer L04 first. Keep the playable Q01 architecture and its quality. Do not make a late week-five decision to claim a one-month schedule.
